Mysterious Rifts are short side fights where you face one of PokeSurvivor's 87 legendaries. This page covers when they appear, what you win, how bonds work, and the terrain and weather in each of the 18 type arenas. For the full list of legendaries, use the [official legendaries page](https://pokesurvivor.com/en/legendaries/).

## How a rift appears and how to enter

When a rift opens, the game shows "A mysterious rift has opened!" and a purple arrow points toward it. Walk into the portal to enter. Inside, you fight one legendary in an arena of its type, with that arena's terrain and weather.

The timing rules, checked in the game client (2026-10-14 build):

- No rift opens in the first 75 seconds of a run.
- No rift opens while a boss is alive.
- No rift opens from minute 28 onward, so rifts never overlap Arceus at minute 30.
- Otherwise the game rolls for a rift every 30 seconds, and the chance grows with each miss. At most 2.5 minutes pass without one, which matches the developer's [news post](https://pokesurvivor.com/en/news/) of 2026-10-12.
- The portal closes after about 45 seconds if nobody enters.

Our tip: you have time to clear the horde around the portal, but not to wander off. If a healing spring is on the way, use it; otherwise head straight in.

## Rift rewards: bonds and ×10 tickets

- **Win:** one bond, plus a 50% chance that the legendary drops a ×10 gacha ticket. All pickups on the field are pulled to you.
- **Lose:** if everyone is knocked out, the rift throws you out with no reward. The run continues.
- **Time limit:** the fight lasts up to 120 seconds.

You don't catch or keep the legendary. The ticket drop was always two tickets before the 2026-10-12 update; it is now a single ×10 ticket half the time. You spend tickets in the region gacha; see [best Pokémon to start with](/best-pokemon/) for how partners and tickets work.

### How bonds work

Bonds came in the 2026-10-11 update. Each legendary you defeat in a rift gives you one bond. A bond links two moves so they fire at the same time from a single slot. You can have up to four active bonds. If you have no second move to link yet, the game saves the bond until you learn one.

A bond frees up a move slot, so you can carry more moves than your slots alone allow. That makes the bond the reward that matters most. Two ideas, both our suggestions rather than tested builds:

- Link your main damage move with one that controls space, such as Bubble (it slows what it touches), so both stay active while you dodge.
- Link a move you're levelling toward evolution with one that is already evolved, so the slot keeps pulling its weight.

## Which legendary you meet

There is no fixed legendary per arena. There are 87 legendaries; the arena matches the legendary's type, and dual-type legendaries pick one of their types at random. Rayquaza (Dragon/Flying), for example, can appear in either the Dragon Sanctuary or Sky Peak. Legendaries you haven't beaten yet are four times as likely to be picked as ones you have.

The pool is uneven across types. Counting the [official list](https://pokesurvivor.com/en/legendaries/) on 2026-10-14, 23 legendaries carry the Psychic type, while only one (Genesect, Bug/Steel) carries Bug. Arceus is on the list too, but its best-known fight is the separate minute-30 event, where it is invincible (news, 2026-10-13). That chase is not a rift.

## Strategy by arena

Terrain and weather below are confirmed from the game client. The move picks are our suggestions, based on the standard type chart the game uses.

### Fire: Igneous Volcano

Lava pools burn you every half second unless your partner is a Fire type, and the sun halves Water moves. That makes the obvious Water Gun pick weaker than it looks here. Ground and Rock moves are not affected by sun, so they are the better counter. Stay on solid ground and only cross lava when you must.

### Water: Sea Grotto

Water pools slow you to 60% speed unless you're a Water type. Rain boosts Water moves ×1.5 and Electric moves ×1.2, so Electric moves are the strongest counter here. Grass moves also work.

### Ice: Eternal Glacier

Ice is slippery and snow wears down and slows anything that isn't Ice type. Fire moves like Ember are the easy answer, with Fighting, Rock and Steel as backups. Start moving earlier than you think before telegraphed attacks, since you'll slide.

### Grass and Bug: Emerald Jungle, Hive Forest

Both have swamp patches that slow you to 45% speed (Water types aren't slowed). There's no weather, so Fire moves like Ember or Fire Blast work at full strength, and Flying moves are good too. Hive Forest only ever has Genesect, which is Bug/Steel, so Fire is by far the best pick there.

### Poison: Toxic Swamp

Poison pools deal damage over time to anyone who isn't Poison or Steel type. Poison resists Grass moves and is weak to Ground and Psychic, so a Confusion user has the edge. All three Poison legendaries are dual-types, so check what you're facing.

### Electric: Volt Plant

Spark tiles hurt every 0.7 s unless you're Electric or Ground type. Rain boosts Water ×1.5, but Water is only neutral against Electric. Ground moves are the only standard counter, and a Ground-type partner also ignores the sparks.

### Ground and Rock: Red Desert, Ancient Quarry

Sandstorm boosts Rock, Ground and Steel moves ×1.3 and wears down other types. The Red Desert also has slowing swamp patches. Water and Grass moves cover both Ground and Rock; they don't get the sand boost, but they hit super effectively.

### Dragon and Flying: Dragon Sanctuary, Sky Peak

The Dragon Sanctuary has lava pools, so Fire types are safest there. Sky Peak has no terrain or weather, which makes it one of the easier arenas. Ice moves are the classic answer to both; against Flying, Electric and Rock moves also work, and against Dragon, Fairy moves do. With 13 Dragon and 11 Flying legendaries, you'll see these arenas often.

### Psychic, Ghost and Dark: Mind Temple, Gloomy Graveyard, Dark Alley

Only the Gloomy Graveyard has weather: fog boosts Ghost, Fairy and Psychic moves ×1.3. Ghost moves are super effective against Ghost, so they get both bonuses there. Bring a Dark or Ghost move against Psychic, and Fighting, Bug or Fairy moves against Dark. The Mind Temple has the largest pool (23 Psychic legendaries), so it's worth having one answer ready.

### Normal and Fighting: Eternal Meadow, Ancient Dojo

The Eternal Meadow has no terrain or weather. Only Fighting moves are super effective against Normal, and Ghost moves don't hit it at all, so lean on your strongest evolved move. The Ancient Dojo has solid pillars to dodge around; Flying, Psychic and Fairy moves are super effective, so Confusion fits well.

### Steel: Steel Fortress

No terrain or weather. Steel resists Grass, and Water is only neutral, so use Fire, Fighting or Ground moves.

### Fairy: Enchanted Forest

A few water pools slow you down. Poison and Steel moves are super effective. Fairy resists Fighting, Bug and Dark moves and is immune to Dragon, so don't count on those here. Ten legendaries carry the Fairy type, many of them dual-types.

## How to beat a rift legendary

The developer's news post of 2026-10-10 says bosses and legendaries have four telegraphed attacks, close in or fight from range, and enrage at half health. It doesn't mention rifts specifically, so we assume the same rule applies to rift legendaries. In practice:

- You have 120 seconds. Learn the four tells early, but don't play so safe that you run out of time.
- Keep moving once it enrages and accept slower damage rather than getting hit.
- Step out of ground markers and damaging terrain as soon as you can.
- An evolved main move makes a big difference, so try rifts after your first evolution if you can. See [How to Play](/how-to-play/), and the [PokeSurvivor tier list](/tier-list/) for which starting moves evolve most easily.
- If you're picking a partner with rifts in mind, the [best first Pokémon guide](/best-pokemon/) compares starting moves.

## Rifts in co-op and on mobile

In a group, everyone enters together, and the rift only fails once the whole team is down. With up to 4 players, spread out so the legendary's attacks can't catch everyone at once. More on group play in the [co-op guide](/co-op/).

On a phone, rifts work the same; see [PokeSurvivor on mobile](/mobile/) for touch controls. Slowing and slippery terrain is harder to handle with a touch joystick, so give yourself extra room before telegraphed attacks.
